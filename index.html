How the peg pyramid works

The board is arranged as a triangle of staggered pegs, with each row wider than the one above it. A ball sent from the top has an even chance of drifting left or right at each peg, and that same split repeats all the way down. When the final peg is passed, the ball settles into one of the pockets along the base, which is always one more than the number of rows.

Because every move is a repeated fifty-fifty turn, most balls settle near the middle and only a few travel all the way to the edge. Mathematicians describe the shape as a Galton board, and the result as a binomial distribution. In casino terms, the payout table follows that curve in reverse: common pockets pay less, while rare edge pockets pay more.
